# Modeling

Numbers for the current model are in [RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md), which is regenerated by `trackmyferry train`.

## 1. Arrival-time prediction

### Problem

At time *t*, a boat matched to trip *T* is at distance *a* along its route. For every downstream
landing *j*, predict when it will arrive, **with an uncertainty range**: the 10th, 50th and 90th
percentiles of the time remaining.

### Labels without ground truth

There's no public record of when NYC Ferry boats actually arrive, so we create it. The stop detector
(`processing/events.py`) watches AIS for a boat slowing below 3 kn inside a landing's 150 m
geofence. On simulated data, where the true times are known, detected arrivals are off by about 5 s
on average.

Training rows come from **replaying recorded feeds through the production pipeline**
(`ml/dataset.py`). Every 30 s, each matched boat produces one feature row per downstream stop: the
exact rows the live service scores. Each row's label is the first detected arrival of the same boat
at that stop. Because the same code builds training and serving features, there's no train/serve
skew.

### Features (`processing/features.py`)

| Group | Features |
|---|---|
| Where it is | metres to the stop along the route shape, stops in between, docked or not, how long it's been docked |
| How it's moving | speed now, mean speed over 2 min |
| Schedule | scheduled time remaining, wait until the scheduled departure (boats hold when early), delay so far |
| Operator | official GTFS-realtime prediction (missing if the feed has none) |
| Water & weather | **tidal current help or hindrance** over the rest of the route: NOAA's Hell Gate prediction × the share of the remaining path aligned with the East River axis (weaker in the Upper Bay), plus wind and rain |
| Dwell | typical dwell at the stops in between (learned online per stop and hour), how much longer than usual this boat has been dwelling |
| Time | hour (sin/cos), day of week, weekend |
| Identity | route and stop (categorical) |
| Physics | free-run time at 20 kn plus 40 s per leg, and a naive estimate combining all of the above |

### Model (`ml/eta_model.py`)

Three histogram gradient-boosted trees (scikit-learn's LightGBM-style implementation) with pinball
loss at q = 0.1, 0.5 and 0.9. Trees can't extrapolate linear trends, so they predict a **correction
to a baseline**: the official prediction when there is one, otherwise the physics estimate.
Quantiles are sorted after prediction so they never cross.

### Calibrated uncertainty: conformalized quantile regression

Quantile trees are usually over-confident. The last three training days are held out. On them we
compute the conformity score *E = max(p10 − y, y − p90)* and widen the band by its 80th percentile
(with the finite-sample correction), separately for 1, 2, 3–4 and 5+ stops ahead (Romano, Patterson
& Candès, 2019). This guarantees about 80% coverage on new data from the same distribution without
retraining. The applied margins are saved with the model and reported in RESULTS.md.

### Evaluation protocol

* **Time-based split**: train on the earliest days, calibrate on the next 3, test on the last 5.
  There's no random shuffling, which would leak the same trips across splits.
* **Baselines**: timetable, the operator's own prediction (falling back to the timetable when
  missing, as an app would), and the physics estimate.
* **Metrics**: MAE, RMSE, 90th-percentile absolute error, bias, and the share within 1 minute,
  overall, by horizon (0–5, 5–10, 10–20, 20+ min) and by route, plus band coverage and width.

## 2. Crowding (`ml/crowding.py`)

**Goal:** the chance a boat is full when it reaches your stop.

1. **Boardings.** A Poisson-loss gradient-boosted model of hourly boardings per (route, direction,
   stop, hour, day of week, month, scheduled departures), fitted to NYC Open Data's hourly
   ridership. Rates are interpolated to the minute between neighbouring hours, so an 8:10 boat sees
   the 8–9 AM peak rather than a flat hourly average.
2. **People already on board.** The boardings at earlier stops, thinned by the chance each rider
   hasn't got off yet. The data has no alightings, so we assume **return-trip symmetry**: where
   people get off in the morning peak mirrors where they get on in the evening peak, and vice versa.
   Without this, morning rush loads were under-estimated by about 40%.
3. **Uncertainty.** Boardings are over-dispersed, so they're Negative Binomial with dispersion
   fitted on training residuals; thinning preserves the NB form. The total (on board plus wanting to
   board) is moment-matched to an NB, and *P(total > capacity)* is reported.

**Known limitation: censored demand.** Ridership counts people who boarded, not people who wanted
to, so on the busiest trips demand is capped at capacity in the training data. The model ranks risky
departures well and is calibrated in the 1–50% range, but under-calls the most extreme rush-hour
crowding. Two ways to fix it: an uncensored demand signal (abnormally long dwells, "boat full"
alerts in the realtime feed), or a censored-likelihood (Tobit-style) fit.

## 3. What's validated, and how

| Component | Check | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Arrival detection (labels) | detected vs true arrival/departure times | `tests/test_processing.py`, RESULTS.md |
| Trip matching | share of underway positions matched to the correct trip | tests, RESULTS.md |
| ETA model | time-split test vs three baselines; band coverage | RESULTS.md |
| Crowding | Brier score vs base rate, AUC, calibration table vs simulated left-behind events | RESULTS.md |
| Live accuracy | `prediction_errors` SQL view joins every prediction to the actual arrival | Grafana, `/api/accuracy` |

## 4. Honest caveats

* The reported numbers are on **simulated** data with a realistic but synthetic fleet, timetable and
  geometry. They show the method works and how much each component contributes. Real numbers come
  from running `trackmyferry record` for a few weeks and retraining.
* The simulator's "official" predictions use a simple rule (schedule plus last observed delay, no
  recovery). NYC Ferry's real predictions may be better or worse, so how much Track My Ferry improves on
  them must be measured on live data.

## 5. Next steps

* **Sequence model.** A GRU or Transformer over the last ~10 minutes of each boat's path (speed
  profile, along-track progress, dwell pattern) as an extra input or a separate quantile head. It
  needs the replay to also store a short track history per row.
* **Uncensoring demand** for crowding, as described above.
* **Online monitoring and retraining.** Alert when live MAE or band coverage drifts (both are already
  on the Grafana dashboard), then retrain nightly on the last N weeks.
* **Better geometry.** Snap AIS fixes to the GTFS shape with a hidden Markov model to make matching
  more robust during GPS noise and coverage gaps.
